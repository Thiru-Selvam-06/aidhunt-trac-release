CORE SECURITY INVARIANTEvery business record belongs to exactly one workspace. A user may belong to multiple workspaces, but a UID never creates permission to cross a workspace boundary. All reads, writes, deletes, sync, cache, search, reports, financial calculations and exports remain scoped to the currently authorized workspace.

This document captures the architecture explicitly established in the design discussion, including the final policy deltas locked after the first Firebase Rules review. It is intended to be handed to Codex as the stable baseline and implemented one area at a time. Older authorization documents and legacy rule models do not override this document.

1. Purpose and Scope
AIDHUNT Trac is an offline-capable, multi-workspace business application in which a single human identity can participate in multiple businesses. The security model must distinguish the person (Firebase Auth UID), the workspace membership, the role within that workspace, and the ownership or attribution of individual records.
What this architecture defines
How a fresh user creates a Firebase account and their first workspace.
How an Owner invites a person who is already a user or has never used the app.
How one OTP represents the invitation and consent handshake for joining a workspace.
How OWNER, PARTNER and OPERATOR roles are assigned and interpreted per workspace.
Exactly what each role can view, create, edit, delete, reverse, collect, use or manage.
How workspace isolation is enforced through multiple layers, including Firestore Rules.
How Partner collection history and financial attribution are preserved.
Current-stage subscription scope: no subscription model restricts user validity, workspace creation, member invitations/additions, or record creation. Any future paid-plan design is out of current scope.
How Room, offline queues and sync preserve workspace boundaries.
What is not assumed
There is no CO-OWNER role.
A Partner lock mechanism for Expenses, Withdrawals, Balance Sheet, Customer Credit or Business Overview is not part of the current model. Partner visibility is currently allowed.
Firestore Rules do not themselves validate SMS OTPs; Firebase Authentication / the trusted invitation flow performs OTP verification.
No subscription or plan-based enforcement is implemented in the current stage. If a subscription/payment model is introduced later, its payment state must be server-owned and the architecture must be explicitly revised; the mobile client must not fabricate paid state.

4.2 Owner member removal and Deleted Users lifecycle
Only the OWNER may remove a workspace member. OWNER cannot change PARTNER <-> OPERATOR, cannot suspend/reactivate a member, cannot transfer ownership, and a workspace has exactly one OWNER.
TWO-STAGE LIFECYCLE: Removing a member from the active workspace is Stage 1 and does not permanently delete business data. It immediately revokes that member’s access to this workspace and moves the member into an Owner-only Deleted Users section under Account. Stage 2 is an explicit permanent purge initiated later by the Owner from Deleted Users. Do not perform the permanent purge merely because the member was removed.
STAGE 1 - REMOVE FROM WORKSPACE: The removed member can no longer enter or perform operations in this workspace. Their existing records remain in the same workspace with original UID attribution. Add an Owner-only Deleted Users section under Account and list the removed member there. In member/history views, retained records linked to the removed member are shown greyed out and read-only where applicable. Grey-out is presentation only and is not a security boundary.
STAGE 1 - FINANCIAL CONTINUITY: Existing customer dues produced by retained Job/Entry records remain active. Current authorized OWNER/PARTNER users may continue to open the due, record a payment, and create new Collection History records against it under normal collection rules. Greyed/read-only applies to the removed member’s retained operational/source record, not to the authorized payment-collection action. Existing collection records remain available at this stage.
STAGE 1 - NO NEW ATTRIBUTION TO REMOVED MEMBER: Active users may continue ordinary workspace operations for themselves or other currently eligible active members, but no new Entry, Expense, Withdrawal, Tractor or Extension may be created for, owned by, or attributed to the removed UID. Do not offer the removed member as an Entry-For, Expense-For, Withdrawal-For or equivalent target, and do not attach new operational records to the removed UID.
STAGE 2 - PERMANENTLY DELETE FROM DELETED USERS: The Owner explicitly selects the removed member in Deleted Users, chooses Permanently Delete Records, and confirms. Delete the eligible data for that member from Firebase/Firestore and the corresponding local Room data in the CURRENT workspace only. Scope is based on creator AND target/beneficiary/financial-attribution identity, not creator alone: an Entry created by the Owner for Partner A is included when entryForUid or financialAttributionUid identifies Partner A; apply the equivalent createdByUid plus expenseForUid/financialAttributionUid and withdrawalForUid rules to Expenses and Withdrawals. Include Tractors/Extensions created by the removed UID, customer dues sourced by purged Entries, and collection records collected by the removed UID or attached to a purged due. Reconcile financial effects before deleting collection records so that a retained due is restored correctly if one of the removed member’s collections is purged from it. Normal in-app collection deletion remains reversal with status=REVERSED; permanent member purge is a separate, explicitly confirmed data-erasure workflow.
WORKSPACE AND QUEUE SAFETY: Permanent purge must never delete the Firebase Authentication account/UID itself or any membership/data in the person’s other workspaces. Cancel or invalidate pending sync operations for the purged entities in this workspace so offline queued writes cannot recreate deleted documents. Process purge in an idempotent, retryable way; keep the member visible in Deleted Users as PURGE PENDING until cloud and local cleanup is confirmed, and report completion only after the scoped purge has succeeded.
SHARED CUSTOMER AND DEPENDENCY SAFETY: Do not delete a Customer that is still referenced by any Entry or other business record retained for another workspace member. Delete it only after dependent records in the purge scope are removed and no retained records reference it. Where an asset record is purged but retained historical records need its identity for display, preserve the historical snapshot/reference required by those records rather than corrupting their history. Scope every query and deletion to the selected workspaceId.

7. Customer Due and Split-Collection Model
Customer: ABC MotorsUnderlying Job / Due: J001Total Due: ₹50,000        |        +--------------------+        |                    |        v                    vCollection #1          Collection #2₹20,000               ₹30,000Collected by OWNER    Collected by PARTNERcustomerId=C001       customerId=C001jobId=J001            jobId=J001Both collections are portions of the SAME customer obligation.
The collection history preserves the collector separately from the creator of the original job. A reversal does not erase history: the Partner's ₹30,000 collection changes to status=REVERSED, ₹30,000 returns to the same customer's outstanding due, and the Owner's earlier ₹20,000 collection remains untouched.
FINAL DELETE LIFECYCLE RULE: Entries, Expenses, Withdrawals, Tractors, Extensions and Customers use hard deletion when DELETE is authorized, subject to dependency protection for shared Customers and historical references. Deleting a Due hard-deletes the underlying Job/Entry that created it. Normal collection deletion is never physical deletion: it changes the collection to status=REVERSED and restores the due. The separate, explicitly confirmed permanent purge from Deleted Users may physically remove collection records within that member’s purge scope only after the financial effects and dependent obligations have been reconciled.
FINAL COLLECTION RULE: Collection reversal retains the collection record with status=REVERSED. Physical deletion is not the financial reversal operation.

9. Subscription (Future Scope; Not Implemented in Current Stage)
CURRENT IMPLEMENTATION RULE: No subscription, paid-plan, account-validity, plan-expiry, member-count, workspace-creation, invitation, or record-creation restriction is implemented in this stage. Users may create workspaces, add members and create records subject only to authentication, workspace membership and role-based permissions. Do not add subscription gates or expiry checks.
FUTURE CONCEPT ONLY: If a subscription model is approved in a future architecture revision, a workspace plan could cover its active members. This concept is not implemented or enforced now.
FUTURE CONCEPT ONLY: A future plan might define personal-plan fallback or a FREE downgrade after coverage ends. This flow is not implemented and has no effect on current access.
FUTURE ONLY: If a subscription model is approved later, workspace coverage may be shared by active members of that workspace.
FUTURE ONLY: A separate member purchase would not be required solely to use a paid workspace, if that future model is adopted.
FUTURE ONLY: Any personal-plan fallback or FREE downgrade requires a separate architecture decision; no expiry or downgrade check applies now.
Workspace access is currently determined by authentication and workspace membership/role. Any future subscription must not itself grant workspace membership.
No subscription state is required or checked in the current implementation. If subscription functionality is added later, its paid state must be server-owned.
FINAL OFFLINE RULE: Every <24-hour permission window is measured operationally from immutable original createdAt; editing never resets or extends it. When an action is performed offline, record its local operation time in the durable pending operation and use it for delayed synchronization. This is the accepted practical model for this app: do not build cryptographic device attestation or an over-engineered clock-proof service. A sufficiently modified device could manipulate local time/state, and that limitation is accepted. Continue enforcing workspace, authenticated UID/session, cached role, creator/target and reference checks; offline support must not become a way to bypass role permissions.
10. Offline, Room and Synchronization
OFFLINE WORKFLOW1. Check the persisted authenticated session and cached workspace/role.2. Check the role permission and perform the local operation in Room.3. Atomically persist the local change and a durable pending operation; show the result as PENDING.4. When connectivity returns, process pending work in small, dependency-ordered groups in the background.5. Recheck cloud authorization and references. Mark only acknowledged operations SYNCED; keep retryable failures pending and expose permanent rejections.
A pending mutation created in Workspace A must never be replayed against Workspace B.
Changing the active workspace in the UI must not rewrite the workspaceId of a queued mutation.
Local reads must be scoped to the active workspace.
Other devices do not see offline-only changes until synchronization occurs.
OFFLINE SESSION: A previously authenticated user must be able to reopen and use the app offline with the persisted authentication session and cached last-known workspace/membership/role context. Missing network alone must not log the user out. First-time Firebase phone/OTP sign-in still requires network and cannot be completed offline.
OFFLINE OPERATIONS: Cached workspace data, lists, reports/calculations and all locally available workflows permitted by the cached role must continue to work offline, including New Entry, Owner-only Historical Entry management, permitted edits/deletes, customer collections/reversals, and withdrawals. Owner and Partner withdrawal behavior must follow the same role targets as online; Operator has no withdrawal access. Offline must not bypass RBAC.
LOCAL AUTHORIZATION: Before changing Room or enqueueing an operation, use the last successfully synchronized membership/role snapshot for the active workspace and apply the same role/ownership/time rules. If the local account/workspace/role context is unavailable, do not guess permissions; defer the operation until a valid context exists.
DURABLE QUEUE: Commit the local business change and its pending queue operation atomically. Keep operationId (for idempotent retry), workspaceId, actorUid, operation type, entity/record identity, sequence/order, recorded local operation time and dependency references. A workspace switch must never re-home a queued operation. A local hard delete must retain a queued delete command/tombstone until cloud acknowledgement.
SYNC PERFORMANCE: Sync runs in the background using the project’s existing worker/queue mechanism. Drain a small bounded set of operations per pass (for example, around 10–20 operations, tuned to measured performance), with limited concurrency and a short yield between passes when needed. Do not push 100 records in one giant upload or dispatch every write simultaneously. Keep network work off the main thread and keep UI interactions responsive; do not build a second synchronization framework.
DEPENDENCY ORDER: Preserve queue order and upload prerequisites before dependent operations (for example, a Job/Entry and its referenced records before a collection against its due; the original collection before a reversal). Retryable operations must have stable identifiers so a retry cannot duplicate a payment, entry or withdrawal.
ACKNOWLEDGEMENT AND RETRY: Mark each operation SYNCED only after Firestore acknowledges that operation. Transient network/server failures remain pending and retry with bounded backoff. A permanent authorization, workspace, validation or conflict rejection must remain identifiable for recovery/diagnosis; never silently discard it or show it as synced. Continue processing independent queued work where dependencies permit. When a member is permanently purged, cancel or invalidate pending operations for affected entity IDs in that workspace and ensure they cannot later recreate purged records.
WORKSPACE-LOCAL RESULTS: Pending offline changes immediately affect the current workspace’s Room-backed UI and calculations. They must not appear in another workspace or be combined with another workspace’s records. Other devices see these changes only after successful cloud synchronization.

END-STATE PRINCIPLEOne person, one immutable UID. Many workspace memberships are allowed. One membership has one role in one workspace. Business records never cross workspace boundaries. Roles remain stable regardless of which member is viewing. Record mutation authority is narrower than visibility. Financial attribution is based on the business relationship and actor, not simply the creator.

Final policy delta: v1.6 clarifies the two-stage Deleted Users lifecycle, UID-based permanent-purge scope, current-workspace-only deletion, practical offline clock limitation, and purge/sync consistency; locked role permissions remain unchanged.
The normal invitation OTP is single-use and valid for 5 minutes. Test-number OTP flows currently have no time limit solely because the real OTP/SMS service is not enabled.
Owner cannot change PARTNER <-> OPERATOR, suspend/reactivate, or transfer ownership. A workspace has exactly one OWNER.
Stage 1 removal revokes membership, moves the member to the Owner-only Deleted Users section, keeps records greyed/read-only, and leaves existing dues collectible by authorized OWNER/PARTNER users.
Stage 2 permanent purge is initiated from Deleted Users and deletes eligible data from the current workspace only, including records targeted to the member as well as records they created; delete dues sourced by purged Entries and reconcile related collections while protecting shared Customers still referenced by retained records.
Member removal is two-stage: Remove from workspace first; permanently delete later from Deleted Users. No automatic or immediate hard purge occurs during Stage 1.
OWNER has 7 report sections; PARTNER has 6: Business Overview, Customer Credit Due, Collection History, Expenses, Balance Sheet and Withdrawal are visible to both; Old Entry Creation is the Owner-only seventh section.
18. Final Locked Decisions
Three roles only: OWNER, PARTNER, OPERATOR.
No CO-OWNER role.
Fresh independent user creates a workspace and becomes OWNER.
Owner invitation uses one OTP sent to the target phone; target sharing the OTP represents consent/handshake.
A target can be brand-new or already have an account/workspace.
An existing user's other workspaces remain unchanged when they join a new workspace.
A new invited user can later create their own workspace and become its OWNER.
Every member sees the same role assignment for every other member; viewer identity does not rewrite role values.
Partner can view shared workspace records on the explicitly allowed surfaces, but view permission never grants edit/delete/reverse authority.
Partner Entry edit/delete: own-created <24h.
Partner Expense edit/delete: own-created <24h.
Partner Tractor/Extension delete: own-created anytime; no 24-hour restriction.
Partner Withdrawal: view all, create only for self, delete own <24h.
Partner Collection: can collect customer due, including Owner-created due; collection stores collectedByUid; Partner may reverse only their own collection <24h.
Split collections retain the same customer identity and underlying job/credit identity.
Owner's earlier collection remains untouched when Partner reverses their later collection.
Owner-created entry for Partner + 'I Paid' attributes the paid/invested amount to the Partner the entry is for.
The same attribution principle is used for Add Expense.
Partner withdrawal/expense target dropdowns are not shown when the target is self-only.
UI hiding is not a security mechanism.
Current stage: no subscription model or subscription-based validity, member-count, workspace-creation or record-creation restriction is implemented.
Future paid-plan sharing/personal fallback is not implemented; it requires a separate explicit architecture revision before development.
Practical offline model is accepted: recorded local operation time supports delayed sync, but it is not tamper-proof; do not over-engineer clock attestation. Offline actions still follow the cached role and workspace permissions.
Workspace isolation applies to read/write/delete/sync/cache/search/report/financial/export operations.
HAND-OFF STATEMENTThis document is the stable architectural baseline for implementation. When Codex encounters older code, legacy rules or older documentation that conflicts with these locked decisions, it should treat the conflict as migration work and preserve the architecture defined here. Changes to locked decisions require an explicit architecture revision rather than an incidental code change.
